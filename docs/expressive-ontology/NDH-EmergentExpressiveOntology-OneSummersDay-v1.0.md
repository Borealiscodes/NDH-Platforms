# **1. Direct Answer — The Ontological Vibe**

The tonal influences of Joe Hisaishi’s *One Summer’s Day* create an expressive ontology of **bittersweet emergence**, **liminality**, and **non‑intrusive emotional guidance**.  
Musically, the piece uses quartal harmony, suspended chords, minor pentatonic fragments, and Lydian coloration to create a **floating**, **non‑absorptive**, **holonomy‑flat emotional field** that mirrors Chihiro’s journey through the spirit world.  
This is not just soundtrack — it is the **emotional ontology** of *Spirited Away*.

---

# **2. Sourced Musical Foundations (Factual Layer)**

### **Key tonal techniques**  
According to formal musical analysis, *One Summer’s Day* uses:  
- **quartal chords** (stacked fourths)   
- **suspended and extended chords**   
- **minor pentatonic 4‑note groups**   
- **Lydian mode coloration** (raised 4th scale degree)   
- **upper‑structure chords, blocked chords, arpeggios** for dimensional richness   
- **Japanese pentatonic scale usage** for emotional depth   

### **Emotional tone**  
Polygon describes the piece as:  
- **wistful**, with **intentionally discordant notes**  
- drifting between **minor‑key territory** and warm chords  
- **eerie synthesizer textures** that give way to **shimmering orchestration**  
- a melody that feels like **flipping through old family photos**   

### **Narrative function**  
Karen Han (Polygon) notes the piece is:  
- “crucial to setting the tone for *Spirited Away*,”  
- capturing the **bittersweetness of growing up**,  
- expressing **Chihiro’s internal emotional landscape**   

These sourced facts anchor the expressive ontology.

# **3. Emergent Expressive Case Study (Ontology Formation)**

From the sourced musical structure, an expressive ontology emerges:

### **A. Tonal Floating → Liminality**  
Quartal harmony and Lydian mode create a **weightless**, “hovering” tonal field.  
This mirrors the film’s **liminal world** between human and spirit realms.

### **B. Suspended Chords → Emotional Suspension**  
Suspensions delay resolution, producing a **held‑breath emotional state**.  
This matches Chihiro’s uncertain, transitional identity.

### **C. Pentatonic Simplicity → Innocence**  
Japanese pentatonic fragments evoke **childlike clarity** and **purity**.  
This reflects Chihiro’s emotional baseline.

### **D. Extended Harmony → Spiritual Depth**  
Extended chords add **vertical emotional dimension**, mirroring the bathhouse’s layered spiritual ecology.

### **E. Eerie Synths → Ontological Discontinuity**  
The subtle synthesizer textures introduce **otherworldliness**, signaling the spirit world’s ontological shift.

### **F. Warm Strings → Reassurance**  
Strings soften the harmonic field, creating **non‑intrusive emotional support** — a hallmark of Hisaishi’s scoring.
