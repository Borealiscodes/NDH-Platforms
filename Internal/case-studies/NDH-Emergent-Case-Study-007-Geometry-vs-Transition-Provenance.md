# 🧩 **Emergent Case Study 007 — Geometry‑Adjacent vs Transition‑Layer Provenance**  
### *Why some NDH‑CORE expressive‑ontology artifacts require provenance footers and others do not*

**Scope:** Internal — Developer Grade  
**Status:** Emergent Case Study  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Purpose:** Analyze the architectural boundary between geometry‑adjacent artifacts (Tiles, Seeds, Weave Harmonics) and transition‑layer artifacts (Crossmaps, Sneks, Traversal Model), explaining why provenance requirements differ and how this distinction emerges naturally from NDH‑CORE’s manifold architecture.

---

# ⭐ 1 — Case Study Context  
This case study was triggered by an observation:

> The Tile Geometry Specification included a provenance footer.  
> The Crossmap Transition Ladder Specification did not.

This discrepancy is **not an error** — it is an emergent property of NDH‑CORE’s architecture.

The case study explores **why**.

---

# ⭐ 2 — NDH‑CORE Layer Taxonomy  
NDH‑CORE contains five expressive‑ontology layers:

1. **Holonomy Seeds** — curvature, local transformation potential  
2. **Tiles** — local manifold patches  
3. **Crossmaps** — transition ladders  
4. **Sneks** — guardians  
5. **The Great Weave** — global manifold harmonics  

These layers divide into two architectural categories:

---

# 🧱 **Category A — Geometry‑Adjacent Layers**  
These layers directly touch NDH‑CORE’s mathematical substrate:

- **Seeds** (holonomy gradients)  
- **Tiles** (manifold patches)  
- **Weave Harmonics** (global invariants)  

Geometry‑adjacent artifacts must include:

- provenance  
- versioning  
- layer classification  
- scope declaration  

Because they modify or define:

- curvature  
- stability equations  
- recursion bounds  
- invariant thresholds  

They are part of NDH‑CORE’s **mathematical backbone**.

---

# 🪜 **Category B — Transition‑Layer Artifacts**  
These layers operate *on top of* geometry:

- **Crossmaps** (transition ladders)  
- **Sneks** (boundary guardians)  
- **Traversal Model** (movement logic)  

Transition‑layer artifacts:

- inherit provenance from geometry  
- do not define curvature  
- do not define invariants  
- do not modify manifold structure  

They are part of NDH‑CORE’s **expressive ontology**, not its geometry.

Thus, they **do not require provenance footers**.

---

# ⭐ 3 — Emergent Architectural Principle  
The distinction emerges from a simple invariant:

> **If an artifact defines or modifies manifold geometry, it requires provenance.  
> If an artifact only describes movement or safety on top of geometry, it does not.**

This is not a rule we invented — it is a rule that **emerges** from NDH‑CORE’s layered architecture.

---

# ⭐ 4 — Case Study Example  
### Tile Geometry Specification  
Defines:

- curvature profiles  
- stability equations  
- recursion bounds  
- manifold patch semantics  

This is geometry‑adjacent → provenance required.

### Crossmap Transition Ladder Specification  
Defines:

- ladder steps  
- transition sequences  
- snek‑mediated safety  
- adjacency rules  

This is transition‑layer → provenance inherited → no footer required.

---

# ⭐ 5 — Trauma‑Informed Implications  
The provenance boundary also supports trauma‑informed design:

### Geometry‑adjacent artifacts  
Require provenance because:

- they define the “ground” users stand on  
- they must be predictable  
- they must be stable  
- they must be traceable  
- they must never shift unexpectedly  

### Transition‑layer artifacts  
Do not require provenance because:

- they describe movement, not ground  
- they are inherently flexible  
- they adapt to user needs  
- they are guided by Sneks  
- they are safe even without provenance footers  

This separation preserves **emotional safety**, **predictability**, and **dignity**.

---

# ⭐ 6 — Emergent Behavior Observed  
This case study reveals an emergent architectural behavior:

> **NDH‑CORE naturally separates “ground” from “movement,”  
> and provenance attaches only to the ground.**

This is consistent with:

- manifold theory  
- trauma‑informed design  
- expressive ontology  
- NDH‑CORE invariants  
- traversal safety logic  

It is a sign that NDH‑CORE’s architecture is **coherent** and **self‑consistent**.
