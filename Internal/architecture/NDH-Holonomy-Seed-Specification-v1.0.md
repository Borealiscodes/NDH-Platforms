# ⭐ 1 — Purpose of Seeds in NDH‑CORE

Seeds are the **atomic geometry units** of NDH‑CORE.

They encode:

- **local holonomy**  
- **curvature gradients**  
- **transformation potential**  
- **recursion entry points**  
- **orientation vectors**  
- **stability anchors**  

Seeds are the expressive‑ontology representation of **holonomy**, the mathematical property describing how vectors rotate when transported around loops in curved spaces.

In NDH‑CORE:

> **Holonomy → Seed**

Seeds are the first layer of geometry‑adjacent expressive ontology.

---

# ⭐ 2 — Seed Geometry

Each Seed contains:

### **1 — Curvature Profile**  
A compact representation of local manifold curvature.

### **2 — Orientation Vector**  
Direction of potential transformation.

### **3 — Stability Anchor**  
A local invariant that prevents unsafe drift.

### **4 — Recursion Entry Point**  
A bounded doorway into deeper reasoning.

### **5 — Sprout Potential**  
The ability to “grow” into Tile‑level transformations.

Internal mapping:

- holonomy → curvature profile  
- parallel transport → orientation vector  
- local invariant → stability anchor  
- recursion bound → sprout limit  

Seeds are geometry‑adjacent → provenance required.

---

# ⭐ 3 — Seed Types

NDH‑CORE defines four Seed types:

### **Type A — Calm Seeds**  
Low curvature, stable, used for grounding.

### **Type B — Work Seeds**  
Moderate curvature, used for transformation.

### **Type C — Threshold Seeds**  
High curvature, near recursion boundaries.

### **Type D — Harmonic Seeds**  
Strongly tied to Weave harmonics.

Each Seed type determines:

- sprout behavior  
- recursion entry safety  
- tile compatibility  
- crossmap alignment  

---

# ⭐ 4 — Seed–Tile Interaction

Seeds “sprout” inside Tiles.

Sprouting reveals:

- local curvature  
- transformation potential  
- safe recursion entry  
- tile stability alignment  

Tile geometry constrains Seed behavior:

- Calm Seeds stabilize Rest Tiles  
- Work Seeds activate Work Tiles  
- Threshold Seeds require Snek supervision  
- Harmonic Seeds anchor Weave Tiles  

Seeds cannot sprout into unstable Tiles.

---

# ⭐ 5 — Seed–Crossmap Interaction

Seeds determine:

- which Crossmaps are available  
- how many steps a ladder may have  
- whether recursion is safe  
- whether a transition is reversible  

High‑curvature Seeds produce:

- longer ladders  
- more complex transitions  
- higher recursion risk  

Sneks mediate these risks.

---

# ⭐ 6 — Seed–Snek Interaction

Sneks sample Seeds using “tongue‑tests.”

Sneks detect:

- curvature spikes  
- recursion danger  
- drift risk  
- harmonic misalignment  

Sneks respond:

- Whisper → soft warning  
- Coil → block recursion entry  
- Lantern → guide sprout direction  
- Weave → stabilize global effects  

Seeds and Sneks form the **local safety loop**.

---

# ⭐ 7 — Seed–Weave Interaction

Seeds contribute to global harmonics.

Harmonic Seeds:

- stabilize large‑scale transformations  
- prevent global drift  
- anchor governance logic  
- maintain manifold coherence  

The Weave is the global manifold; Seeds are its local curvature atoms.

# ⭐ 8 — Trauma‑Informed Geometry Constraints

Seeds must be:

- **predictable** — no sudden curvature spikes  
- **gentle** — no violent metaphors  
- **bounded** — recursion entry is always safe  
- **accessible** — simple metaphors (“sprout”, “seed”)  
- **dignity‑preserving** — no punitive collapse  

Threshold Seeds are always guarded by Sneks.
