## **1. Purpose**

The Governance Constitution defines:

- the philosophical foundation of Orbital governance  
- the ethical basis for naming invariants  
- the cognitive‑ergonomic justification for documentation rhythm  
- the architectural rationale for provenance boundaries  
- the inclusivity principles guiding dyslexia‑aware naming  
- the cross‑repo necessity of unified governance  

It answers the question:  
**Why does Orbital govern NDH naming and documentation at all?**

---

## **2. Foundational Principles**

Orbital governance is founded on four constitutional principles:

### **1. Stability**  
Naming must be stable across time, repos, and layers.  
Stability reduces cognitive load and prevents architectural drift.

### **2. Predictability**  
Documentation rhythm must be predictable.  
Predictability supports dyslexic developers and reduces interpretive friction.

### **3. Provenance**  
Naming must reflect lineage and architectural ancestry.  
Provenance prevents cross‑layer contamination and preserves NDH’s structure.

### **4. Inclusivity**  
Naming and documentation must be safe for dyslexic developers.  
Inclusivity is a constitutional requirement, not an optional courtesy.

These principles justify Orbital’s authority.

---

## **3. Constitutional Mandate**

Orbital is constitutionally mandated to:

- define naming invariants  
- enforce documentation rhythm  
- maintain provenance boundaries  
- ensure accessibility and cognitive safety  
- unify cross‑repo naming behavior  
- prevent architectural drift  
- preserve NDH’s multi‑layer coherence  

This mandate is permanent and binding.

---

## **4. Architectural Justification**

NDH is a multi‑layer system:

- **NDH‑CORE** — operator ergonomics  
- **NDH‑TIDS** — topological routing  
- **Emergent** — interpretive scaffolding  
- **Orbital** — governance and invariants  

Without Orbital:

- naming families would drift  
- acronym rhythm would collapse  
- provenance boundaries would blur  
- dyslexic developers would face unpredictable naming patterns  
- documentation would fragment  
- NDH would lose structural coherence  

Orbital governance is necessary for NDH’s stability.

---

## **5. Ethical Justification**

Orbital governance exists to protect:

- dyslexic developers  
- cognitively diverse contributors  
- maintainers navigating complex naming systems  
- future readers of NDH documentation  

The Constitution asserts:

> Naming must never harm the developer who reads it.

This is the ethical core of Orbital governance.

---

## **6. Cognitive‑Ergonomic Justification**

Dyslexic developers require:

- rhythm stability  
- shape‑consistent acronyms  
- predictable naming families  
- clear provenance markers  
- consistent documentation flow  

Orbital governance ensures these conditions are met.

---

## **7. Constitutional Powers**

Orbital is constitutionally empowered to:

- define naming families  
- enforce naming invariants  
- enforce rhythm rules  
- enforce shape‑consistency  
- enforce provenance anchors  
- enforce accessibility rules  
- enforce schema compliance  
- enforce validator compliance  
- enforce automation compliance  

These powers are justified by the Constitution’s principles.

---

## **8. Constitutional Limitations**

Orbital must not:

- alter runtime semantics (NDH‑CORE)  
- alter topological semantics (NDH‑TIDS)  
- alter interpretive semantics (Emergent)  
- introduce naming families outside the **Naming Invariant Ledger**  
- violate dyslexia‑aware safety rules  

Orbital governs naming and documentation — not execution.
